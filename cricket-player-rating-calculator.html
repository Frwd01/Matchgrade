Cricket player rating calculator

A batter's rating and a bowler's rating are built from completely different numbers — runs and strike rate versus overs, wickets, and economy. MatchGrade's cricket calculator uses a separate model for each role so the score actually reflects what that player was on the pitch to do.

How cricket ratings are calculated

Every cricket rating starts at 6.0. From there, the model splits by role:

RolePrimary stats
BatterRuns, balls faced, strike rate, boundaries, dismissal
BowlerOvers, runs conceded, wickets, economy, maidens
All-rounderBlend of batting and bowling with lower individual baselines
Wicket-keeperBatting line plus catches, stumpings, byes conceded

Worked example — bowler

A bowler sends down 8 overs, concedes 32 runs, and takes 3 wickets. Economy of 4.0 is well under the 7.0 baseline, adding roughly 1.0 to 1.2 points; the three wickets add close to 2.7 points at 0.9 each. Starting from 6.0, this lands around 9.0 to 9.5 — an excellent, near match-winning bowling performance.

Worked example — batter

A batter scores 45 off 38 balls with 5 fours. Runs above the 30-run baseline add about 0.7, strike rate above 110 adds a modest amount, and the boundaries add roughly 0.3 — landing around 7.3 to 7.7, a very good batting contribution without a standout strike rate.

How soccer ratings are calculated

Every rating starts at 6.0, the same neutral baseline used by professional match-rating services — a did-their-job performance, nothing special either way. From there, the score adjusts based on how the entered stats compare to what's typical for the selected position, scaled to a 90-minute equivalent so short appearances aren't unfairly judged.

Context matters too. Goals conceded pull a defender's or goalkeeper's rating down more than a striker's. A red card is a heavy penalty regardless of position. A clean sheet is rewarded for defensive roles. Winning the match adds a small bonus; losing subtracts one — small, because the individual stat line should carry most of the weight.

Worked example

A striker plays the full 90 minutes, scores two goals, records one assist, and takes four shots with three on target. Starting from 6.0, the two goals add roughly 2.1 points using diminishing returns per goal, the assist adds around 0.9, and the shot accuracy adds a small bonus for conversion — landing the final rating around 8.6 to 9.0, in the "excellent" tier. A defender with three tackles, two interceptions, and a clean sheet in a 1-0 win would land closer to 7.2 to 7.6 — solidly "very good" without the ceiling a goal-scoring game gives an attacker.

Frequently asked questions

How is a soccer player rating out of 10 calculated?

MatchGrade starts every player at a 6.0 baseline, representing an anonymous, did-their-job performance. The score then moves up or down based on stats compared to what is typical for that position, plus context like goals conceded, cards, and result.

What stats do I need to rate a soccer player?

It depends on position. Goalkeepers need saves and shots faced. Defenders need tackles, interceptions, and clearances. Midfielders need pass accuracy and key passes. Forwards need goals, assists, and shots on target.

Does the calculator account for minutes played?

Yes. Stats are scaled to a 90-minute equivalent so a player coming on as a substitute isn't unfairly judged for having fewer total actions. Very short cameos are pulled gently back toward the neutral 6.0 baseline.

Can I rate goalkeepers with this calculator?

Yes. Goalkeepers use a save-ratio model (saves divided by shots faced) plus clean sheets, crosses claimed, and penalty saves, instead of the outfield stats used for other positions.

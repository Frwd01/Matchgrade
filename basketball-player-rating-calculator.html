A 20-point game means something different for a point guard than for a center. MatchGrade's basketball rating calculator weighs points, rebounds, assists, shooting efficiency, and turnovers differently depending on position, so the final rating reflects what that role is actually supposed to contribute.

How basketball ratings are calculated

Every basketball rating starts at 6.0, an average, did-their-job performance for a starter-level workload. The score then adjusts based on how the entered box score compares to typical production for the selected position, scaled to a 32-minute baseline.

PositionPrimary stats
Point guardPoints, assists, turnovers, field goal %
Shooting guardPoints, field goal %, three-point %, assists
Small forwardPoints, rebounds, assists, field goal %
Power forwardPoints, rebounds, field goal %, blocks
CenterPoints, rebounds, blocks, field goal %

Optional stats for a sharper rating

Steals, three-point percentage, free throw percentage, and plus-minus can all be added for more precision. Plus-minus in particular captures on-court impact that raw counting stats miss, and carries a modest weight in the final score.

Worked example

A point guard plays 32 minutes with 14 points, 8 assists, 2 turnovers, and 46% shooting. Starting from 6.0, the above-average assist count and low turnover rate each add roughly 0.4 to 0.5 points, and the shooting efficiency adds a smaller amount — landing in the 7.2 to 7.6 range, a very good floor-general performance.
